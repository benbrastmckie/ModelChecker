### F2. The principle at the family level, stated (question (a))

**Settled Event Verification.** Fix `M`, `sigma`, `i`, an anchor `x`, and an operator `O` from the world-quantifying family. For an `x`-anchored family `pi` with convex domain:

- `L_O(pi)` (settling): for every world-history `beta` with `pi <= beta`, `M, beta, x, sigma, i |= O phi`. `L-_O(pi)` with `=|` (falsity).
- `I_O(pi)` (the operator's own clause at the candidate): the truth clause of `O phi` read with `pi^top` in the history slot, where `pi^top` agrees with `pi` on `dom(pi)` and is the full state elsewhere. `I-_O(pi)` with the falsity clause.
- `V_x(O phi) := closure_ext( Min { pi : I_O(pi) and L_O(pi) } )`, `F_x(O phi) := closure_ext( Min { pi : I-_O(pi) and L-_O(pi) } )`,

with `Min` under Evolution Parthood on `x`-anchored families and `closure_ext` closure under extension-fusion (`@def-extension-fusion`; all members share the anchor, so it is always defined, and by `@lem-anchored-union` convex domains stay convex).

What plays the role of `I` and `L`, operator by operator:

| `O phi` | `I_O(pi)` reads | `L_O(pi)` | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| `A []-> B` | for every `A`-verifier `pi'`, `rho in [pi^top|_{dom pi'}]_{pi'}`, `beta` with `pi' + rho <= beta` and `pi' + rho` a thread: `B` at `beta` | every `beta >= pi` is a `A []-> B`-history | family `ILMC` |
| `[] phi = top []-> phi` | `top`'s verifiers include every world state `w` as `pi_w`; `[pi^top(x)]_w` gives `beta(x) = w`; so `I` is `[] phi` for every `pi` | `[] phi` | `{pi_null}` iff `[] phi` |
| `S phi` | histories `alpha` with `alpha(x) >= pi(x)`: `phi` | same | minimal parts of world-states settling `S phi` |
| `phi == psi` (and `<=`, `<=|` via `@def-essence-ground`) | nothing (model-level) | nothing | `{pi_null}` iff the identity holds |
| `<>->`, `<>`, `Will`, `Could`, ... | by negation duality (`@def-event-connectives`) | | falsifiers of the dual |

Three definitional adjustments make `I` statable; none changes `@def-counterfactual-truth`:

1. **General bounding family.** `@def-maximal-compatible-subevolutions` fixes "an evolution `tau`". Reading the counterfactual clause at `pi^top` needs `[f]_{pi'}` for an arbitrary function `f : X -> S` (clauses (a)-(d) make sense verbatim). This is the exact parallel of `@def-maximal-compatible-parts` taking any `s : S`, which is what lets the state-level `I(t)` be defined for every state, possible or not. `@def-evolution-maximality` need not be generalized: if `[f]_{pi'}` is empty, `pi'` contributes vacuously to `I`, and `I` only selects.
2. **Containment form of equality-pinned clauses.** `@def-stability-truth` quantifies over `alpha` with `alpha(x) = tau(x)`. At a world-history this is equivalent to `alpha(x) >= tau(x)` by `@def-world-state` maximality, and only the containment form is meaningful at a non-maximal candidate. Read with equality, `I_S(pi)` is vacuously true for every non-world `pi(x)` and `I-_S(pi)` vacuously false, so the falsifiers of `S A` come out as whole world-states (`{0:b.d}`, `{0:c.d}`, `{0:b.c.d}`) while its verifiers are minimal parts -- oracle E2 first run, `...-e235.txt`. With containment both polarities are minimal parts (`{0:a}` / `{0:d}`, `...-e2s.txt`). `@lem-necessity-semantics` and `@def-altworlds` are already in containment form.
3. **Padding.** Outside `dom(pi)` the candidate says nothing. Padding by the null state and keeping clause (c) (the witness `rho` must be a thread) forces `rho` to be the constant null family by the Nullity constraint whenever an antecedent verifier sticks out, which discards `pi`'s content entirely for that verifier. Padding by the full state is the faithful lift: `[top]_t` is the set of world states containing `t` (`r in [top]_t` is `t`-compatible and maximal, any world above `r + t` is `t`-compatible and above `r`, hence equals `r`), so at a padded point the imposition is free, exactly as `Alt(null, a)` is the set of `a`-worlds at the state level. The oracle implements both readings (`padding="full"` / `"restrict"`, the latter being "impose only antecedent verifiers whose domain fits inside `dom(pi)`"). They agree on verifiers in every experiment and differ on falsifiers of tensed-antecedent counterfactuals (E5: co-settlers `{0:c}` under full padding; `{0:c,1:a}`, `{0:c,1:b}`, `{0:c,1:c}`, `{0:c,1:d,2:*}` under restrict, because the existential `I-` needs an antecedent verifier that fits). Full padding is recommended; restrict is recorded.

Availability of the two extremal steps:

- **Fusion closure** is available and sound: if `pi_1, pi_2` settle then `pi_j <= pi_1 + pi_2 <= beta` for every history above the fusion, so the extension-fusion settles. E1 (Closure, same-domain pointwise fusion) is the same-domain instance. Closure adds only fusions of settlers, so soundness of `V` is unconditional.
- **Minimality** is definable (Evolution Parthood is a partial order on `x`-anchored families) and, because domain inclusion is part of the order, it *derives domain-exactness*: the least family that settles a CF-constituent counterfactual has domain `{x}`. But existence of minimal elements is not automatic; see F5.3.

### F3. The null-family convention is a computed case (question (b))

For `==`, `T` reads nothing, so `I` and `L` are the model-level condition and `IL` is either every `x`-anchored family or none. The least `x`-anchored family under Evolution Parthood is the singleton `pi_null` (the domain must contain `x`; the value at `x` must be a part of every value), so `Min IL = {pi_null}` and `closure_ext` of a singleton is itself. This is `@def-event-cf-base`'s `==` case via `@def-propid-verification` verbatim: `dom(pi) = {x}` and `pi(x) = nullstate`. The same computation gives `[] phi` the null family (F2 table), which the manual currently gives no verifiers at all, and `S phi` the null family exactly when `phi` holds at every world (E2: `S(A v ~A)` has `V = {pi_null}`). The manual's own sanity check at 03-dynamics.typ:1259 -- `Alt(tau, pi_null)` collapses to the stability class so `(phi == psi) []-> chi` reads `S chi` -- is thereby the general fact that a null-verified antecedent minimizes its consequent (task 185 report 02, section 5.3), now stated once for `==`, `[]` and every other history-independent operator.

Oracle E2 (window `{0,1}`, 4-atom frame, `...-e2s.txt`): `(A == A)`, `[](A v ~A)`, `[]((A v B) v (C v D))`, `S(A v ~A)` each return `V = {pi_null}`, `F = {}`; `(A == B)` and `[]A` return `V = {}`, `F = {pi_null}`.

So the chapter can state one principle and *derive* `@def-propid-truth`'s "context-independent" remark from it: the null family verifies what every family settles.

### F4. Alignment with the untensed fragment: the family recipe restricted to singleton domains is `ILMC`

For a counterfactual with CF constituents, `T` reads only `beta(x)`, so `L(pi_t)` is "every world above `t` is a `T`-world" (every world occurs at `x` in some history, `@cor-occurrence`) and `I(pi_t)` is the state-level `I(t)` by `@cor-zero-duration-collapse`. Hence the domain-`{x}` slice of the family `IL` is the state-level `IL` in every frame, and its minimal members are minimal there. Oracle E1 (4-atom frame with worlds `a.b`, `a.c`, `d.b`, `d.c`; `|A| = <{a},{d}>`, `|B| = <{b},{c}>`): state-level `ILMC` `V = {b}`, `F = {c, a.d, a.c.d}`; the family recipe at windows `{0}` and `{0,1}` gives exactly the embedding on domain `{0}`, sound and sufficient in both polarities, and no realizable member with a larger domain. This is the counterfactual analogue of `@lem-cf-alignment`, and it is what makes `@prop-cf-conservativity` extend from truth to verifiers. Oracle E4 repeats the check on report 02's certified 8-atom F3 frame (`...-e4.txt`): at windows `{0}` and `{0,1}` the realizable minimal settlers are `{0:a.p'}`, `{0:a.q'}`, `{0:a.b}` and the realizable minimal co-settlers `{0:a'}`, `{0:p.q}`, `{0:p'.q}`, `{0:p.q'}`, `{0:b'}`, the minimal elements of the state-level `ILMC` sets of task 185 report 01 F5, so the refutation of Reading I and its repair transfer to the manual's own machinery unchanged.

Two qualifications. First, the slice equality is a theorem; the *absence* of larger-domain realizable minimal settlers is a property of memoryless frames. In a frame whose task relation constrains cross-time combinations, a family `{x: t, z: u}` can settle a CF-constituent counterfactual although `t` alone does not (the value at `z` rules out the bad worlds at `x` through coherence). Such settlers are legitimate temporal exactness (`@rem-no-domain-closure`) and are invisible at the state level; no certified frame of that kind exists in the manual or the prior reports (see "Where the evidence does not decide"). Second, the vacuous members differ: at the state level a vacuous `IL` member is an impossible state; at the family level it is a family below no world-history, which need not be pointwise impossible (F9).

### F5. The strict collapse and its escape (question (d))

**F5.1 Measured.** Oracle E3, window `{0,1}`, ten consequents (`A, B, C, D`, their negations, `A & C`, `B v D`), three antecedent shapes, both paddings (`...-e235.txt`, `...-e2s.txt`):

Identical for both paddings. The `ILMC` rows are the base counterfactual profile (settled result 1, task 185 report 01 F7); the `ILC` rows are Reading W's logic (report 02 F6). The last column is Round 1's Reading B, refuted for both.

**F5.2 Why the escape is available.** Reading W and `ILC` include every true world-history in `V_X` (a history `tau` has `I(tau) = T(tau)` and settles trivially), and imposing a world-history on anything yields that history (report 02 M1), so `X []-> C` ranges over all `X`-histories. `Min` removes `tau` from `V_X` whenever some proper part of it settles, and then `Alt(tau, pi)` for a thin `pi` is a genuine imposition. Nothing about families blocks this: E3 shows it at a tensed antecedent (`(F A) []-> B`, whose realizable minimal settler is `{0:b}`) and at a stability antecedent.

**F5.3 What blocks it in general: existence of minimal settlers.** `closure(Min IL)` is sound unconditionally but sufficient only if some minimal settler lies below every history at which `X` is true. The set of settlers below `tau` is nonempty (`tau` itself), and a minimal element exists iff descending chains are bounded in `IL`. The pointwise meet of a chain of settlers need not settle (a history above the meet need not be above any member), so nothing in the frame supplies minimal elements. At the state level this is an issue only for infinite lattices; at the family level it arises over `Q = Z` with a finite lattice, because domains descend: with `X = G p`, the settlers below `tau` on the forward ray can be thinned point by point without end when the lattice allows (values fixed, domain a ray, no finite-domain settler exists at all). So the recipe needs a **Settler Minimality** principle -- every settler of a world-quantifying `O phi` has a minimal settler below it -- which is the descending twin of `@def-maximal-constraint` and `@def-evolution-maximality`, with the same motivation ("no strictly decreasing series with no bound"). Where it holds automatically: `S` finite and settling dependent on a bounded window of times (every CF-constituent counterfactual, `S phi` with CF `phi`, every bounded-interval tense antecedent over `Q = Z`), which covers every frame the manual's countermodels use and every oracle run here. Where it is a genuine assumption: dense `Q`, ray-dependent antecedents, infinite lattices. Without it the honest statement is: `C2` is sound at a recipe-governed antecedent `X` at `tau` iff a minimal settler of `X` lies below `tau`; `ILC` (report 02's Reading L in imposition form) is the fallback with unconditional sufficiency and the strict logic.

### F9. What the family level costs that the state level did not

**F9.1 Pointwise Exclusivity fails; history-form Exclusivity holds.** `@def-anchored-event` E3 requires some shared time at which a verifier and a falsifier are incompatible. In memoryless frames every pointwise-possible family is realizable, so restricting E3 to realizable members restores it ("E3 pointwise (realizable members): True" in every run); in a temporally constrained frame a pointwise-possible family below no history is a vacuous settler and co-settler with no impossible value anywhere, and even realizable members can be pointwise compatible yet jointly unrealizable. The tense fragment satisfies pointwise E3 only because its falsifiers are fat (whole rays); the recipe's are thin on both sides.

**F9.2 Settler Minimality** (F5.3): a new principle or a conditional sufficiency.

**F9.3 Non-convex minimal settlers.** `Min` under domain inclusion can return non-convex domains; the coherence filter then makes them vacuous as antecedents. In every oracle run the non-convex minimal members were unrealizable (E5: `{0:null, 2:a.b.c.d}`), so nothing was lost; whether a *realizable* minimal settler can have a non-convex domain in a constrained frame is untested. ### F12. The recipe on a temporally constrained frame (open items 1 and 2)

**Certified frame.** `baselines/02_constrained-frame-oracle.py`'s `forbidden_transition_relation`, applied to `frame_small` (atoms `a b c d`; worlds `a.b`, `a.c`, `d.b`, `d.c`), forbids the single world-to-world transition `a.b -> a.c` at every nonzero duration, leaving the duration-uniform schema unchanged everywhere else. `certify_constrained_frame` checks, as a run-time assertion rather than a comment: (1) the forbidden pair is genuinely excluded by the frame's own relation at every duration in the window; (2) Nullity survives (the null state is `d`-persistent for every duration); (3) the pair is not already excluded by the base schema for an unrelated reason at window `{0,1}` and `{0,1,2}` (a genuine restriction — at window `{0}` the check WARNS that the single-time schema already excludes it vacuously, since no nonzero-duration transition is exercised there, and the experiment at that window is reported as such rather than treated as meaningful). **The certificate refuses a false claim**: certifying the SAME pair against a frame whose relation was built WITHOUT the constraint reports `CERTIFICATE FAIL` and the caller aborts before running any experiment (`baselines/02_constrained-frame-oracle-output-c12.txt`, the `[PERTURBED CERTIFICATE]` run).

**Experiment C1 (multi-time realizable minimal settlers; non-convex realizable minimal settlers).** At windows `{0,1}` and `{0,1,2}`, the unique realizable minimal settler of `A []-> B` remains the single-time state `{0:b}` — **no multi-time realizable minimal settler appears**, contrary to F4's prediction of a settler shaped `{x:t, z:u}` with `t` not itself a state-level settler. The reason is structural, not a search failure: `A` and `B` are both CF-constituent (state-level exact) formulas, so `alt(g, pi')` (the imposition step inside `I_{[]->}`) restricts its search to `dom(pi') = {0}` regardless of what larger domain the outer candidate settler has (`Model.alt`'s `X := fr.dom(pi)` is the ANTECEDENT VERIFIER's domain, never the candidate settler's), and `B`'s truth is read only at the anchor. A transition forbidden strictly after the anchor therefore cannot affect whether `A []-> B` is true or false at the anchor, so it cannot recruit a later time-point into a minimal settler for THIS antecedent/consequent shape. **This is a recorded negative result for the specific CF-constituent schema tested, not a refutation of F4's prediction in general**: the mechanism that WOULD produce a multi-time settler needs a tensed consequent or antecedent (so truth genuinely depends on more than the anchor value) together with a constraint that bites between the times the tensed operator quantifies over; this frame/antecedent combination was not built to test that case, and doing so is named as the deciding test below rather than attempted further, per the plan's own contingency (do not keep hunting past the timing budget). **Experiment C2 (pointwise-compatible settler/co-settler pair with no common history).** At window `{0,1}`, the prediction is confirmed: `V = {0:b}` (the settler) and `F = {0:a, 1:a.c}` (a co-settler) are pointwise compatible on their shared domain `{0}` (`compat(b, a)` holds — `a` and `b` are compatible, `a.b` is possible) yet **no world-history contains both**, because `F`'s continuation to `a.c` at time 1 requires the very transition `a.b -> a.c` this frame forbids, and no OTHER history extending `{0:a}` reaches `1:a.c}` while agreeing with `{0:b}`'s own requirement that `b` be part of the world at time 0 (`a.b` is the only world containing both `a` and `b`, and from `a.b` the forbidden transition blocks reaching `a.c`). Consequently **pointwise E3 among realizable members fails on this frame** (`False`, versus `True` on the unconstrained memoryless schema at window `{0}` in the same run), while history-form E3 is unaffected (nothing changes about which V/F pairs share a history, since the pair that fails pointwise E3 already shares no history by construction). This directly confirms F9.1's prediction: pointwise E3's failure at realizable, non-impossible members is a genuine feature of temporally constrained frames, not an artifact confined to vacuous (below-no-history) members as in the memoryless measurements of round 1.

**Soundness and sufficiency, both polarities, are unaffected**: `True`/`True` at every window tested, exactly as in the memoryless schema (F4/F9's soundness argument uses only that a settler's every extension makes the formula true, which no restriction of the relation can break — restricting a relation can only shrink the alternative set, never introduce an alternative that violates a settled requirement). **The F5.1 validity profile spot-check** (identity, MP, strict-to-cf, cf-to-strict, AS, might-identity, at consequent `B`, the pinned antecedent's own consequent rather than the full 10-consequent sweep) is identical at both windows and qualitatively matches round 1's memoryless-schema shape: identity, MP, strict-to-cf and might-identity valid; cf-to-strict fails; AS valid at this particular consequent (consistent with round 1's "fails 2/10" — `B` need not be one of the two failing consequents). The frame's constraint does not visibly perturb the nested-logic profile at this antecedent shape, consistent with F5.2's account of the escape mechanism (minimality removing total settlers) being independent of which transitions the relation forbids.

**What this decides, precisely.** Open item 1 (multi-time realizable settlers) is answered NEGATIVELY for CF-constituent antecedents and consequents on this frame — a recorded negative result, per the plan's contingency, not a refutation of F4's general prediction. Open item 2 (pointwise E3 among realizable members) is answered POSITIVELY: it fails on a genuinely constrained frame, exactly as F9.1 anticipated, closing that item. ### F14. Settler Minimality, padding, and what the evidence still does not decide

**The principle, as it now stands in the manual (superseding F5.3's proposal-form statement).** The manual has already adopted it (F11's preamble; task 406, `@def-minimal-settler`), stated beside `@def-maximal-constraint` and `@def-evolution-maximality` as the third member of the same existence-axiom family:

> **Minimal Settler Constraint.** For every world-history `tau`, time `x`, and formulas `phi_DT`, `psi` such that `phi_DT []-> psi` is true at `(tau, x)`, the set of `<=`-minimal `IL`-families `pi` with `pi <= tau` and `dom(pi)` convex is nonempty.

This is narrower in STATEMENT than F5.3's general "every settler of a world-quantifying `O phi` has a minimal settler below it": it names only `[]->`. It is not narrower in EFFECT, because `[]`, `<>`, `<>->` are abbreviations that unfold to `[]->` (`@rem-box-diamond-inherit`) and `stably` receives its own instance of the identical `V1`/`V2` schema (`@prop-stability-non-null`), so every world-quantifying operator's `IL`-family is an instance of the one notion the axiom quantifies over. **The conditional fallback**, exactly as F5.3 stated it and as the manual's own C2 row records (`11-proof-theory.typ`, the discharge condition): `C2` is sound at a recipe-governed antecedent `X` at `tau` iff a convex-domain minimal settler of `X` lies below `tau`; without the axiom, `ILC` (no minimality step) is the unconditionally-sufficient fallback, at the cost of the strict-conditional collapse (F5.2).

**Whether Phase 1 found a BimodalLogic constraint that supplies, corroborates, or is merely analogous to it.** Corroborates, not supplies — F11's first correspondence row and its answer to question (i): no BimodalLogic axiom has Settler Minimality's *content* (a directed-family-intersection condition is a different mathematical object from a minimal-element-of-a-formula's-settler-poset condition), but `@rem-minimal-settler-third-adoption`'s own stated rationale for adopting an axiom rather than deriving existence — because the concrete model (`DefaultState := Set String` in Lean, a complete lattice admitting infinite descending chains) would falsify a derived-from-well-foundedness argument — is *exactly* the methodology BimodalLogic's `Saturation`/`Serial` already use for the same reason, and BimodalLogic's finite-carrier theorems (`saturation_of_finite`, `exists_uniform_radius_of_finite`) independently confirm the SPECIFIC claim that finiteness makes the axiom's content automatic. The manual's own adoption decision is therefore doubly grounded: by its own argument, and by an unrelated formalization needing the identical move for the identical reason.

**Non-vacuity: a descending chain with no minimal member, informally sketched in the continuous candidate model.** `@rem-threading-general-open`'s own candidate model — states are the closed subsets of `RR` ordered by inclusion, a complete lattice — supports the construction F5.3 named, though only as an illustration riding on a model the manual itself records as unverified at exactly the constraints (Limit, the relativized Spherical) this construction would need to be checked against. Sketch: let the exact-verifier set of an atom `p` be `V_p := { [-1/n, 1/n] : n = 1, 2, 3, ... }` (closed under fusion/union, since `[-1/n,1/n] union [-1/m,1/m] = [-1/min(n,m), 1/min(n,m)] in V_p`), and deliberately exclude `{0}` from `V_p` — a free choice of interpretation, since nothing in the framework's exact-verification axioms forces a verifier set to be closed under arbitrary intersection. Consider the family `pi_n` with a ray domain `[x, infinity)` and constant value `[-1/n, 1/n]` at every point. Each `pi_n` is a candidate settler of `G p` ("p always going to be true"): if the frame supports persisting at a fixed non-minimal verifier value across the whole ray (plausible in this candidate model's task relation, though not one of the three properties the manual has actually verified for it), then `I(pi_n)` and `L(pi_n)` both hold for every `n`, and `pi_{n+1} < pi_n` (a strictly descending chain under Evolution Parthood, since `[-1/(n+1), 1/(n+1)] subsetneq [-1/n, 1/n]`). The chain's pointwise meet is the constant family at `{0}` — but `{0} not in V_p` by construction, so the meet is not itself `p`-verifying and is not a settler; **no minimal element of the chain exists**. This is the mechanism F5.3 named (a value chain shrinking toward a limit the interpretation withholds from the verifier set) rather than a formally checked theorem, exactly as the plan's own task asks: it is **not executable at the oracle's scale** (the oracle's states are finite bitmasks with no infinite descending chains available at all, and the candidate model itself lacks a verified Limit/Spherical to certify the construction rigorously).

**The automatic region, re-stated in light of Phase 3.** F5.3's automatic region is: `S` finite and settling dependent on a bounded window of times. Phase 3's constrained frame stays inside this region on both counts — `frame_small` has 16 states (finite) and every experiment ran at windows of size at most 3 — so C1/C2's results are consistent with (not a counterexample to) automaticity: BimodalLogic's `exists_uniform_radius_of_finite`/`saturation_of_finite` (F11) both show finiteness alone forces the extremal-element existence the axiom asserts, independent of which relation governs the finite carrier, which is exactly why forbidding one transition in Phase 3 changed WHICH settlers/co-settlers existed (C1, C2) without ever threatening existence of a MINIMAL one — the well-foundedness argument that makes minimality automatic on a finite lattice does not care about the relation's shape at all (`sInter_nonempty_of_directed_of_minimal`'s proof, F11, "indifferent to the kind of member"). The genuine test of non-automaticity needs an infinite state lattice or a dense duration order, neither of which the executable oracle can represent; the manual's own axiom is therefore doing real work only outside the region every measurement in this task (and round 1) has been able to reach.

**D2 (padding), closed.** Report 01's open item 4 asked which exact falsifier of "if `A` were going to happen, `B` would" (a tensed-antecedent counterfactual) the manual wants: the co-settler `c` alone (full padding — outside `dom(pi)` the candidate constrains nothing, so imposing `t` on the full state yields exactly the worlds containing `t`, the direct lift of the state-level clause) or `c` together with a way of `A` happening at the padded points (restrict padding — only antecedent verifiers whose domain fits inside `dom(pi)` are imposed). **Decision: full padding**, for the reason report 01 already gave and this task's F12 does not disturb: it is the unique reading under which `@def-maximal-compatible-subevolutions`'s clause (a) (`rho <= pi|_X`, no constraint outside `X` beyond (a) and (c)) is read literally rather than silently strengthened, and it is the reading `@rem-no-domain-closure`'s domain-exactness remark already commits to at the state level (padding by anything less than the full state manufactures domain material the clause does not need, exactly the objection `03-dynamics.typ`:746 raises against a DIFFERENT but structurally identical padding temptation for the antecedent's own domain). Restrict padding remains recorded as the analyzed alternative, not adopted, per D2's original text.

**D3 (Settler Minimality: axiom vs. conditional), current judgment.** The manual has already made this decision (axiom, `@def-minimal-settler`) — this is a fact this task records rather than a choice it makes. What remains genuinely open, for a reader who wants to reconsider it: the axiom's cost is a fourth existence-postulate of a form no automatic-region argument yet covers outside finite/bounded-window frames (this task's own Phase 3 measurement stayed inside that region, so it does not test the cost); the conditional-sufficiency alternative's cost is that `C2`'s soundness becomes formula-and-history-relative rather than unconditional at the recipe's own antecedents, reopening exactly the qualification `@thm-soundness` currently avoids by having the axiom.
