## Key Decisions Made

- **`finalize_certificate()` allocates a witness lasso for every boxed closure member
  up front, unconditionally** -- not only for boxes that end up guessed false. The guess is a
  free Z3 Boolean decided at solve time, so which boxes end up false cannot be known at
  constraint-emission time; allocating unconditionally (main lasso `0` plus one witness lasso
  per distinct `Box` argument in the closure) matches report 01 section 4.4's "at most one
  more than the number of boxes" and lets `max_witnesses` force sharing via
  `WitnessRegistry.allocate_witness_lasso`'s existing round-robin. A witness lasso allocated
  for a box that ends up guessed *true* is harmless: it still must satisfy (C1)/(C2) like any
  other lasso in the family, and the solver is free to make its labels agree everywhere with
  the box faithfulness "everywhere" clause.
- **No incremental (per-call) constraint emission was implemented, despite D6 saying
  local coherence "can be emitted lazily."** That phrasing is permissive, not mandatory --
  witness-lasso allocation (needed for local coherence/fulfilment on non-main lassos, and for
  box faithfulness) cannot happen until the closure is fully known anyway, so doing *all*
  constraint emission in one place (`finalize_certificate`, called once, idempotent) is
  simpler and equally compliant. Do not try to split local coherence out into
  `premise_behavior`/`conclusion_behavior` in a later phase without a concrete reason (e.g. a
  measured performance need) -- it adds real complexity (partial-closure bookkeeping) for a
  permissive-only requirement.
- **`true_at`/`false_at` do not register newly-seen formulas into `_known_closure`.** Only
  `premise_behavior`/`conclusion_behavior` do (matching D6's own naming of exactly these two
  methods as the closure-discovery sites). `true_at` is for later phases' use against an
  *already-finalized* closure (printing, the re-check hook) -- registering there would let a
  post-finalize call silently grow `_known_closure` without ever re-running
  `finalize_certificate`, creating an unconstrained (and therefore misleading) fresh Z3
  Boolean. If a later phase ever needs `true_at` on a formula outside the original
  premises/conclusions' closure, that is a sign something upstream is wrong, not a case to
  paper over here.
- **`verify_model` was deleted, not kept.** It was unused outside `core.py` itself (confirmed
  by grep) and its role -- checking premises true / conclusions false in a found model -- is
  superseded by Phase 12's re-check hook, which checks the much stronger (C1)-(C4) rather than
  just (C4).

## What NOT to Try

- Do not restore `self.M`/`world_function`/`truth_condition`/`task_rel` etc. onto
  `BimodalSemantics` "to keep old callers working a little longer" -- that is exactly the
  compatibility-layer approach the plan's clean-break framing rules out. Callers in
  `operators.py`, `examples.py`, `semantic/model.py`, `semantic/proposition.py` and the oracle
  are fixed by their own phases (14, 16, 12/13, 11, 20), not by re-adding retired attributes.
- Do not try to make `true_at`/`false_at` recurse through `sentence.operator.true_at(...)`
  the way the retired encoding did -- the whole point of D5's translate-then-lookup design is
  that local coherence (a `finalize_certificate` constraint, not a Python-level recursion)
  already ties every compound formula's bit to its constituents' bits. Reintroducing recursion
  here would silently duplicate what the Z3 constraints already enforce and risks the two
  disagreeing.
- Do not attempt to make the full bimodal suite green before Phase 13. Use the Phase 9
  verification tier (grep for deleted-method callers, the 14 new unit tests) as this phase's
  actual gate, and trust the plan's own red-period framing for the suite as a whole.
